**Runner-up:** `random_forest` — CV RMSE 0.622

### Justification
Gradient boosting (n_estimators=500, max_depth=4, lr=0.05, subsample=0.8) achieves the best CV RMSE 0.5753 and R² 0.763, beating random forest (RMSE 0.622, R² 0.724) by ~7.5% and crushing the ridge baseline (RMSE 0.847, R² 0.48). The huge linear-vs-tree gap is consistent with the data profile: only MedInc has a strong linear correlation (0.682) with the target, while the dominant remaining signal lives in non-linear interactions of Latitude/Longitude (coastal/urban premiums) which only tree ensembles can capture — ridge's high fold variance (r2_std 0.15) confirms it cannot handle this spatial structure. A tuned GB (depth 4) marginally improved on the depth-3 GB (0.575 vs 0.590), within one std (0.063), so the two are statistically tied; I picked the deeper variant as the nominal winner. Lasso was skipped (only 8 non-collinear features, no selection benefit over ridge), and a single decision tree was skipped as random_forest strictly dominates it on a 1.6k-row training set.

### Caveats
(1) Capped target: max is exactly 5.0 ($500k cap in the original California housing data). All tree models will systematically underestimate true high-end values because they cannot extrapolate beyond the training ceiling, and CV RMSE near the cap looks artificially good. A log or Box-Cox transform of the target (skew 0.93) — or modelling censored observations with a Tobit-style approach — is the most likely next accuracy gain and was not tested here. (2) Spatial generalisation: Latitude/Longitude are raw features; random k-fold CV mixes regions across train and test, so reported RMSE is optimistic relative to deploying the model in a region under-represented in training. A spatial/grouped CV (e.g., by lat-long blocks) would give a more honest estimate, and engineered spatial features (distance to coast, distance to SF/LA, cluster IDs) are the most likely untapped lift. (3) Hyperparameter search was deliberately narrow — only one GB variant was tuned; a proper grid over learning_rate, max_depth, min_samples_leaf, and n_estimators (with early stopping) could squeeze further gains. (4) Small dataset (n=2000): fold-to-fold variance is non-trivial (GB rmse_std 0.063 ≈ 11% of the mean), so RMSE differences smaller than ~0.04 between models are not statistically meaningful. (5) No held-out test evaluation was used in selection — winner is chosen on CV alone, which carries some selection bias.

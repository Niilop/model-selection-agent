- **Rows:** 2000

## Recommendation
**Winner:** `gradient_boosting`  
**Params:** `{'n_estimators': 500, 'learning_rate': 0.05, 'max_depth': 3, 'subsample': 0.8, 'random_state': 42}`  
**CV RMSE:** 0.6882  
**R²:** 0.6122  
**Training time:** 11469 ms

**Runner-up:** `gradient_boosting (n_est=300, lr=0.05, max_depth=4, subsample=0.8)` — CV RMSE 0.6918

### Justification
The dataset has 2000 rows, 8 features, and a moderately right-skewed target (skew=0.93) that appears clipped at 5.0. Only MedInc shows a strong linear correlation with the target (0.68); other features — including Latitude/Longitude — have weak linear correlations but encode strong non-linear spatial patterns. This profile predicts that linear models will plateau and tree ensembles will dominate, which is exactly what the baseline showed: linear/ridge/lasso clustered around RMSE 0.80, while gradient boosting (0.7226) and random forest (0.7264) led. I therefore skipped further tuning of the linear family and the single decision tree (RMSE 0.878 — clearly underfitting) and focused tuning effort on GB and RF. The baseline GridSearchCV grid for gradient boosting only varied learning_rate, leaving substantial headroom in n_estimators, max_depth, and subsample. Tuning these jointly produced a clear winner: n_estimators=500, learning_rate=0.05, max_depth=3, subsample=0.8 reached CV RMSE 0.6882 and R² 0.6122 — a 4.8% RMSE improvement over the baseline GB and a +0.046 R² gain. The shallow depth (3) plus stochastic subsampling (0.8) and a halved learning rate combats the heteroskedastic, capped target by averaging many weak learners. Random forest variants (best 0.7247) could not match this — boosting's bias-reduction is more effective here than RF's variance-reduction. Three nearby GB configurations all landed within 0.69–0.70 RMSE, confirming the optimum is robust and not a fluke. Training time (~11.5s) is acceptable for a 2000-row dataset.

### Caveats
1) The target's apparent cap at 5.0 was not modeled explicitly — a censored regression (Tobit) or transformation might help further but is outside the candidate set. 2) Lat/Long were used as raw features; engineered spatial features (e.g., distance to coast, clustering) could lift any tree model meaningfully. 3) I did not tune RF deeply across more leaf/depth combinations; given the gap to GB (0.72 vs 0.69) it is unlikely but not impossible RF could close it. 4) The R² standard deviation across folds is high (~0.11), indicating the test-fold accuracy varies considerably — performance on a new geography may differ. 5) No held-out test set was used; reported metrics are 5-fold CV only. 6) Gradient boosting is not parallelizable across trees, so if training latency matters at much larger N, a histogram-based booster (LightGBM/XGBoost) would be preferable.

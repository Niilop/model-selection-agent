# Regression Model Selection Report

- **Rows:** 2000
- **Features:** 8

## Agent Recommendation
**Winner:** `gradient_boosting`  
**Params:** `{'n_estimators': 300, 'max_depth': 4, 'learning_rate': 0.05, 'subsample': 0.8, 'random_state': 42}`  
**CV RMSE:** 0.6918  
**R²:** 0.6075  
**Training time:** 8361 ms

**Runner-up:** `random_forest` — CV RMSE 0.742

### Justification
Gradient boosting with depth=4, learning_rate=0.05, subsample=0.8, and 300 trees achieved CV RMSE 0.6918 (R² 0.608), beating random forest (0.7420) by roughly 1.5 std and the ridge linear baseline (0.8122) by ~15%. The data characteristics drove this choice: MedInc dominates linear correlation (r=0.678) but the remaining features have weak linear signals (max 0.155), so the predictive juice has to come from non-linear interactions — particularly Latitude × Longitude geography combined with MedInc. Ensembles confirmed this: ridge and a single tree both lagged badly (RMSE 0.81–0.93), while bagged/boosted trees jumped to 0.69–0.74. Boosting edged RF because sequential residual fitting captures the geographic gradient more efficiently than RF's averaging on this small (2000 row) dataset. The subsample=0.8 stochastic variant not only had the lowest mean RMSE but also the lowest cv_rmse_std (0.035) and r2_std (0.116), indicating better fold-to-fold stability — important given the spatial heterogeneity. I skipped plain linear (ridge with α=1 already represents this family well), lasso (only 8 features, no need for L1 selection), and didn't tune decision_tree further (single trees were strictly dominated by both ensembles).

### Caveats
Several honest limitations: (1) **Capped target at 5.0** — the original $500k ceiling means tree models systematically underpredict the most expensive homes; CV RMSE near the cap is artificially flattered. A log or Box-Cox transform on the (right-skewed, skew=0.93) target should be the next experiment. (2) **Spatial generalisation risk** — Latitude/Longitude are present, and r2_std of 0.12 (vs cv_rmse mean 0.69, ~17%) indicates folds differ meaningfully, likely because they span different geographic regions. The model may not generalise well to held-out coastal or northern regions. Engineered spatial features (distance to coast, k-means cluster ID, distance to nearest metro) are the most likely untapped performance gain. (3) **CV optimism** — I selected hyperparameters by looking at 5-fold CV scores, so the reported 0.6918 is mildly optimistic; a held-out test set or nested CV would give a more honest estimate. (4) **Limited search** — I evaluated 4 GB variants and didn't grid-search exhaustively; deeper trees, different subsample rates, or lower learning rates with more estimators might shave another 1-2%. (5) **Small dataset (2000 rows)** — the gap between RF and GB could shrink or reverse on a different random split, and any model with high capacity risks overfitting that I haven't fully stress-tested.

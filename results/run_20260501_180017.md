### Justification
The data profile shows the hallmarks of a problem where boosted trees should win: only one feature (MedInc, r=0.68) is linearly informative, while Latitude/Longitude carry strong non-linear/interaction signal that linear models cannot exploit. Ridge confirmed this with a weak CV RMSE of 0.847 (R²=0.48) and a very high R² std of 0.15 — the linear hyperplane simply cannot bend around California's geography. Moving to tree ensembles cut RMSE dramatically: Random Forest reached 0.636 (R²=0.71), and Gradient Boosting reached 0.583 (R²=0.756), an 8% further reduction with lower fit time than RF. I tested two GBM configurations: a compact (200 trees, depth 3, lr=0.1) and a deeper/slower one (400 trees, depth 4, lr=0.05). Their CV RMSEs (0.583 vs 0.580) differ by far less than one std (~0.06), so they are statistically tied; I recommend the compact variant because it trains in ~40% of the time with no meaningful accuracy loss — a clean diminishing-returns case. I skipped Lasso because with only 8 features and no obvious noise variables, L1 selection adds nothing over Ridge, and I skipped an unconstrained Decision Tree because the depth-8 variant already showed it underperforms the bagged/boosted ensembles by a wide margin (RMSE 0.77 vs 0.58).

### Caveats
1) **Capped target**: median_house_value tops out at exactly 5.0 ($500k). All tree models will systematically underestimate true values above the cap, and CV RMSE looks artificially good near the ceiling. A log or Box-Cox transform won't repair the cap, but censored regression (e.g. Tobit) or simply dropping/flagging capped rows is the principled next step. 2) **CV optimism**: I selected GBM hyperparameters by inspecting 5-fold CV scores, so the reported 0.583 is mildly optimistic — a held-out test or nested CV would likely add 0.01–0.03 RMSE. 3) **Spatial generalisation**: Latitude/Longitude are present, and the ~10% relative CV RMSE std (0.06/0.58) suggests folds covering different California regions perform differently. Predictions outside the training footprint (e.g. a new county) are not validated. The biggest untapped gain is almost certainly **spatial feature engineering** — distance to coast, distance to nearest major city, or k-means cluster IDs over Lat/Lon — which would let even simpler models capture the geography GBM is currently learning implicitly. 4) **Search breadth**: I did not tune `min_samples_leaf`, `subsample`, or try XGBoost/LightGBM/HistGradientBoosting; a small additional gain (1–3% RMSE) is plausible but unlikely to change the ordering. 5) **Small N (1,600 train rows)**: results carry a non-trivial CV variance; the gap between GBM and RF (≈0.05) is real (~1 std), but the gap between the two GBM variants is not.
